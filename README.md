# aad-pricer

A C++23 Monte Carlo pricer for path-dependent options (Asians, barriers, digitals), with its own tape-based adjoint algorithmic differentiation (AAD): one backward pass gives the price and every first-order Greek. It is measured against published targets, each with its source in [BENCHMARKS.md](BENCHMARKS.md), and against bump-and-revalue and a hand-written adjoint on the same random numbers.

The emphasis is on checking the numbers. Every comparison against a reference value was registered, with its tolerance, before the run that judged it, and a failed comparison stays failed. A fault-injection runner plants one bug at a time to prove each check can fail. The results tables here are generated from stamped measurement files. The headline table below shows chosen rows, met and missed; under it, a generated count names every target judged on each host, met, missed, met in part or not counted, each linked to its section of BENCHMARKS.md.

It also fits an SVI volatility surface to one trade date of TAIFEX index-option settlement prices and prices off it, and has a QuantLib comparison bench and a Metal GPU pricer. At commit 8bb2116 on `linux-zen1`, every documented command is measured and in `results/`: the implied-vol bench, the SVI fit, the eSSVI and local-vol reports, and the quote sensitivities with their timing and their bump-and-recalibrate check, which fails there as registered. Five of them ran in a later run with the host's scheduled timers paused: the quote-sensitivity report and its check (`qs-report`, `qs-check`), never started in the first run, and three per-kernel runs whose first runs were withdrawn for other activity on the host. On `mac-m4max`, 24 bench files at 8bb2116 and the Metal table at c5f3473 are in `results/`, and the QuantLib accuracy run is curated into `references/`. 19 timed steps of that batch were withdrawn for other activity on the Mac or a missing process record, and each ran again once on 2026-11-26: 12 of those reruns stand and are in `results/` (ten per-kernel runs, the implied-vol bench and one QuantLib speed run), and 7 were withdrawn a second time for other activity on the Mac and get one more run later (`lw-worst`, three per-kernel runs, two QuantLib speed runs and the Metal timing). Until then the Mac's `lw-worst` file is still its stage 3b run at d1596e0.

## What is here

- A platform layer for arm64 and x86-64: CPU information, timers, pinning, performance counters, floating-point state.
- Google Benchmark and HdrHistogram harness code, and a results-file writer that stamps each run with its host, compiler and flags.
- Own `exp`, `log` and AS241 inverse normal in `src/math/`, so libm differences do not reach results.
- The production scalar pricer in `src/pricer/`: arithmetic and geometric Asian, European, digitals, and barriers with a Brownian-bridge weight, one-step survival, a hard indicator or smoothing, on Philox draws keyed on path and step, multi-threaded with a fixed block order.
- The tape in `src/ad/`: struct of arrays, one statement per operation, one tape per worker thread, with no allocation after warm-up. A hand-written adjoint of the GBM Asian is the baseline it is measured against. Second-order Greeks by forward over reverse, and SIMD lanes with a vector tape.
- Bump and revalue on common random numbers, with step sizes from a committed h study, and an AAD benchmark that checks every gradient inside the run before timing it.
- Closed-form and finite-difference oracles, with the tolerance rule implemented once and every comparison fixed in `references/comparison_plan.csv`. Measured run 1 ran the comparisons runnable in Phase 2 on the reference kernels (`references/measured_results.csv`). Measured run 2 ran all of them on the production pricer and tape (`references/measured_results_run2.csv`); its two failures are in the headline and in `docs/NOTES.md`, "Stage 8b: measured run 2". Run 1 is kept beside run 2, unchanged.
- A raw SVI fit to the TAIFEX settlements, an arbitrage-free eSSVI surface beside it, local volatility off the surface, and sensitivities of prices to the calibration settlements through the fit. The surface reaches 33 days only. The registered bump-and-recalibrate check fails in the reported runs at 8bb2116: on `mac-m4max` on 20 of its 3,420 required comparisons, all of them prices on the two barriers, and on `linux-zen1` on 24 of 3,420, 20 prices on the two barriers and 4 parameter comparisons (`BENCHMARKS.md`, `SVI-10`). Stage 7b's iteration run, not a result, had failed on 20 of its 1,080 price comparisons, all on the barriers (`docs/NOTES.md`, "Stage 7b results").
- A Metal GPU backend in `src/metal/`, built on Apple when Metal is found: float32 prices only, for the GBM arithmetic Asian and the one-step-survival barriers. AAD does not run on the GPU. The GPU computes prices and nothing else, and every Greek comes from the CPU, in double precision, by the tape or by bump and revalue. Its kernel source, `src/f32/float_kernels.hpp`, also runs on the CPU as a float32 line, and both are compared with the double pricer on the same truncated uniforms.
- A QuantLib comparison bench in `bench/quantlib/`, behind `AAD_PRICER_BUILD_QUANTLIB_COMPARE`.
- cp950 and TAIFEX file readers, and libFuzzer targets on every parser, decoder and pricer entry point, plus a tape fuzzer against finite differences.
- A fault-injection runner that plants one bug at a time in a copy of the code, to show each oracle and check can fail.

## Build and run

You need CMake 3.25 or later, Ninja, Python 3 and a C++23 compiler. The toolchain comes from the environment (`CC`, `CXX`, and `SDKROOT` on macOS). `clang-format` and `clang-tidy` must be LLVM 23.1.4, and the scripts refuse any other version. Third-party dependencies are pinned archives, fetched into the build tree with their hashes checked. QuantLib is off unless `AAD_PRICER_BUILD_QUANTLIB_COMPARE` is set. The Metal backend, `AAD_PRICER_ENABLE_METAL`, is on for Apple when an Objective-C++ compiler and the Metal framework are found, and off elsewhere. Its tests skip, with the reason, only on a host with no Metal device, and a skipped test still fails `scripts/check.sh` unless it is named.

```sh
cmake --preset release
cmake --build --preset release
ctest --preset release
```

| Preset | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `release` | Optimised, baseline SIMD |
| `debug` | No optimisation |
| `asan-ubsan` | ASan and UBSan |
| `tsan` | TSan |
| `fuzz` | libFuzzer targets with ASan and UBSan |
| `release-gcc` | Optimised, with g++, Linux only |
| `release-avx2` | Optimised, AVX2 with FMA, x86-64 only |
| `release-avx512` | Optimised, AVX-512, x86-64 only. Build only: it has no test preset |

Fetch the data before running the real-file tests. Without it they skip, and `scripts/check.sh` counts a skipped test as a failure.

```sh
scripts/fetch_data.sh            # download the pinned captures and verify their sha256
scripts/fetch_data.sh --verify   # verify what is on disk, with no network
```

Run every check, one after another:

```sh
scripts/check.sh
```

Each step prints one PASS, FAIL or SKIP line and writes a log under `build/logs/check/`. There are 15 steps: the nice level, a check with no build that every fault's edits still apply to the tree, format, clang-tidy, the repo scan, build and ctest for `release`, `debug`, `asan-ubsan` and `tsan`, a timed run of every fuzz target, the measured oracle plan compared with `references/measured_results.csv`, the README headline and `RESULTS.md` compared with a fresh generation, data verification, actionlint, and fault injection last. The fault-injection step runs against the committed `HEAD`, so uncommitted edits do not reach it. Every step runs even after a failure. `AADP_JOBS` sets the jobs, at most 6 and by default 6, and `AADP_FUZZ_SECONDS` the seconds per fuzz target, by default 60. A skipped test or a step that could not run is a failure unless `--allow-skipped-tests` or `--allow-skip-step` names it. `--only <step,...>` runs part of the list and always exits non-zero, so a partial run can never read as a pass. Git gives a command it runs from a hook or from `git rebase --exec` its repository-location variables (`GIT_DIR`, `GIT_INDEX_FILE` and the others `git rev-parse --local-env-vars` names). Every script, test and tool here that starts git removes them first, through `scripts/git_environment.py`, `scripts/git_environment.sh` or `read_provenance`, so git acts on the repository the code names, never on the caller's. The ctest `aadp_git_environment_script` runs the tests and checks that start git with those variables pointing at a scratch repository, and fails if that repository changes.

Other scripts:

- `scripts/fetch_tools.sh` installs the pinned actionlint into `build/tools/`, checked by sha256. With `--llvm` it also installs LLVM 23.1.4, which is how CI gets its compiler.
- `python3 -I scripts/scan_repo.py` fails if a tracked file holds a home path, an IP address, the current user or host name, an agent notes file, or a raw line of the market data. The exceptions are listed at the top of the script.
- `scripts/verify_commits.sh <range>` builds and tests each commit of a range in an export under `build/commit-check/`. It never checks out a commit. Not every commit in this history passes it: [`docs/FAILING_COMMITS.md`](docs/FAILING_COMMITS.md) lists the 81 of 225 through 2444dc8 that fail on the Linux host, by full hash, with what fails.
- `python3 -I scripts/run_tests.py fuzz --seconds <n>` is the fuzz step's runner. A long run is cut into slices of at most 300 s (`--slice`), each its own libFuzzer process on the target's one corpus, so a target that starts threads cannot reach libFuzzer's memory limit through the sanitizer runtime's per-thread bookkeeping. Each slice has a hard limit, and a hang is killed and recorded as timed out; `--wrapper <program>` runs each slice through a lock wrapper, and `--only <target>` picks targets. It and the ctest runs point the sanitizers at the `llvm-symbolizer` beside the build's compiler, so a report cannot hang on the system's own symbolizer; on macOS each program they run gets a dSYM bundle first, so that symbolizer can name lines and inlined frames. The design and its cost are at the top of the script.

## Fault injection

A check that cannot fail proves nothing. `scripts/fault_injection.py` plants one bug at a time, from `faults/manifest.json`, and shows that the oracle or check aimed at it fails for the stated reason. There are 603 faults: 66 against the oracles, 506 against the checks and 31 against the fuzz targets. Each is applied to a copy under `build/fault`, never to the working tree. The unmutated copy must pass first, and the mutated run must fail with output matching the fault's reason. Failing for some other reason, such as a compile error where a test failure was expected, counts as not caught.

```sh
python3 -I scripts/fault_injection.py --list                 # print the faults
python3 -I scripts/fault_injection.py --only oracle-philox   # run one fault
python3 -I scripts/fault_injection.py --jobs 6 --output <file>   # run all of them
```

It needs the toolchain in the environment, as the build does. `--kind` runs one kind, and `--source <dir>` mutates an export with no `.git`. The schema and the verdicts are described at the top of the script, and the runner's own tests are in `faults/test_fault_injection.py`, which ctest runs (`data_fault_injection_script`). `scripts/check.sh` runs every fault as its last step. `--static` builds nothing: in under a second it checks that every fault still applies, each edit's file there and each `find` text matching exactly once, and `scripts/check.sh` runs it as its second step and CI in its repo-scan job, so a fault that a code change left stale is named at once.

Reports are in `results/<host>/fault-injection-<time>.txt`, with the commit, a scope line and one line per fault. Those committed reports are from Phase 2, when there were 41 faults. On the Mac all 41 were caught. On the Linux host 29 ran and all 29 were caught. The 9 fuzz faults and 3 sanitizer faults were left out there, because sanitizer and fuzz runs stay on the Mac. The latest full `scripts/check.sh` run, at 638f69a on the Mac on 2026-11-26, passed all 15 steps and caught all 598 faults. The Mac slept twice while its fault step ran. 182 of the faults ran across a sleep, counting the short dark wakes inside it as sleep, and 33 of them overlap the time it was really asleep. 49 of the 182, each one whose catch could rest on a clock or a time limit, were run again with the Mac awake and caught for the same reason; the other 133, 10 of the 33 among them, were not run again: one is a build fault, caught by the compiler's error, and the source of the test or check that caught each of the other 132 was searched, and none reads a clock, sleeps, or has a time limit, a deadline or a file time (`docs/NOTES.md`, "The closing full check and the Mac's sleeps"). An earlier full run of the faults pinned to 5e4fad8 caught 364 of its 365 and found one stale, `svi-fit-svi11-root`, whose `find` text 6a21a31 had rewritten; it is re-pointed and caught, and the static check above came from it. The five added since 638f69a, with the refusal of a negated verdict word, the verdict readers' unit test and the runner's tests in ctest, were run on their own and caught. `docs/NOTES.md` gives each stage's fault results. What the faults found is in `docs/NOTES.md`, under "Fault injection results".

## Results

Results tables come only from `aadp_results_table`, which reads the raw results files, and are never typed by hand:

```sh
build/release/bin/aadp_results_table results/                # print the tables, one per host
build/release/bin/aadp_results_table --docs . --write        # regenerate RESULTS.md and the headline above
build/release/bin/aadp_results_table --docs . --check        # fail if either differs from a fresh generation
```

`--docs` reads `results/headline.csv`, which says which result answers which target and the target's threshold, the target headings in `BENCHMARKS.md`, every file in `results/<host>/`, and the measured oracle runs in `references/`. It holds no number of its own. A row's value comes from the latest run of its workload on its host, in the build it names if it names one (`<workload>@<build>`, the files' `simd_cmake_target`). No file holds numbers from two hosts, and the tool refuses a file filed under another host's directory. The plain table leaves out a fault-injection report in a host directory, judged by the report's first line; `RESULTS.md` lists those reports in their own table. `results/linux-zen1/` holds the reported run at 8bb2116, and `results/mac-m4max/` the batch of 2026-11-18 (its bench files at 8bb2116 and the Metal table at c5f3473) and the reruns of 2026-11-26 from the same two commits, with only `lw-worst`'s file still from d1596e0, each beside harness self-tests and fault-injection reports.

## Data

The option chain is TAIFEX TXO, European options on the TAIEX, with the matching futures file. Read this before using anything derived from it:

- **These are exchange settlement prices, not tradable quotes.** TAIFEX's rule is that settlement is the last trade, and if there was no trade in the final 15 minutes, TAIFEX decides it. At least 73.7% of all regular-session rows and 52.0% of out-of-the-money rows are exchange-set, and for the 202506 expiry it is 100%. The file has no trade times, so "market-observed" is only an upper bound.
- **It covers one trade date, 2024-11-15.** No stability test is possible.
- **The usable surface reaches only 33 days.** The market-anchored expiries are 5, 12 and 33 days out, so pricing off it is limited to short maturities. Anything longer-dated would be fitted to exchange-set marks and labelled that way.

What remains doubtful, none of it resolved in the data's favour:

1. The licence chain is reasonable, not express: Securities and Futures Bureau, then TAIFEX's authority, then the licence. The listed provider is the Bureau, not TAIFEX.
2. TAIFEX's own terms are ambiguous. Clause 3 exempts data it authorised data.gov.tw to offer, but its next sentence asks for prior written consent to quote or reprint, and the English terms have no exemption at all. The raw file is kept unmodified and the source is cited. This is a reading, not legal advice.
3. Internet Archive's Terms of Use could not be read, because the page is a JavaScript shell. Its developer guide requires a descriptive User-Agent on automated requests, which the fetch script sends. The `id_` URL modifier the pinned links use is observed behaviour, not documented.
4. There is a single point of failure. There is one capture, and if it goes, calibration cannot run until a mirror or a new snapshot is chosen. The fetch script has no fallback source on purpose.
5. The licence covers the open file only. Venue POST quotes were a diagnostic and must not feed calibration.
6. One date only, as above.

The raw files are never committed. `scripts/fetch_data.sh` downloads the Internet Archive captures named in `scripts/data_manifest.txt` and verifies their sha256. `docs/NOTES.md` has the full analysis under "Option chain". Attribution for the data and the third-party build dependencies is in [`NOTICE`](NOTICE).
